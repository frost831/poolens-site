The passport is not another CRM. It is the proof layer at the equipment pad.

Route and business systems are where companies schedule, bill, message, and manage accounts. SplashLens focuses on the stop itself: what was seen, what was checked, what evidence exists, and what a human can hand to a manager, customer, trainer, distributor, or support desk.

Capture

Stop-level evidence

Readings, photos, model labels, visible symptoms, codes, parts, and notes gathered while the technician is still on site.

Remember

Service memory

Keep pool-specific context close to the workflow so the next visit does not restart from scratch.

Explain

Readable summaries

When available, AI-assisted summaries can turn raw field notes into a draft packet for human review.

What a conservative workflow looks like.

The passport does not diagnose, approve work, guarantee fitment, or replace a professional. It makes the evidence legible so the next decision starts from better information.

1

Identify the stop

Select or name the pool, then capture the visit reason and current condition.

2

Collect proof

Save readings, photos, equipment labels, visible codes, and notes before memory fades.

3

Review the summary

Use AI-assisted wording when available, but keep a human in the loop before sharing or acting.

4

Export or hand off

Share a clean packet with a manager, customer, support desk, trainer, or route system as needed.

Built to sit beside the tools teams already use.

SplashLens can support a company that already uses Skimmer, Pool Brain, Jobber, ServiceTitan, notes, texts, or paper. The promise is narrower and more practical: better field evidence and clearer service memory.

Good fit

  • Pool stops where proof gets lost between visits.
  • New tech training and callback review.
  • Parts, labels, photos, and symptoms before ordering.
  • Customer-facing visit explanations that need reviewable detail.
  • Facility or support workflows where a packet beats a vague call.

Not a replacement for

  • Route management, billing, dispatch, or full CRM.
  • Medical, legal, code, or compliance authority.
  • Manufacturer manuals, warranty approval, or official diagnostics.
  • Licensed electrical, gas, structural, or regulated work.
  • Human judgment before a customer-facing summary is sent.

Reference-only positioning: local code, facility policy, manufacturer documentation, qualified professional judgment, and company procedures still control. AI-assisted summaries can be useful drafts, but they are not guarantees.
