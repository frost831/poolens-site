Identify a pool salt cell

A salt cell is identified from the controller label, cell-body markings, unions, cable connector, and cell length. A body photo by itself is not enough to order a replacement, and this page lists no part number.

What the existing proof library asks for

The public PartSnap library uses a faded salt-cell label as the example: unions, cable connector, controller model, cell length, and flow-switch context narrow the family without pretending certainty. Existing site references cover Hayward AquaRite / TurboCell messages, Pentair IntelliChlor winterizing, and Jandy TruClear check-cell prompts. Those pages are clues, not a cross-brand parts interchange.

  1. Photograph the controller label and the cell label, even if the cell label is faded.
  2. Photograph unions, the cable connector, and flow direction or a flow switch if one is installed.
  3. Record cell length and any molded characters that are still readable.
  4. Keep the packet as a possible family until the current diagram confirms the cell.

No part number on this page

Do not infer a cell size from the brand name alone. Confirm the controller family and the cell marking against the current manufacturer diagram before ordering.

Questions

Is a salt-cell photo enough to order a replacement?

No. The PartSnap proof library says a cell-body photo by itself is usually not enough. Capture the controller label, cell markings, unions, cable connector, and length.

Does this page list a salt-cell part number?

No. SplashLens does not publish a replacement part number for Hayward, Pentair, Jandy, or any other salt cell. Match the installed labels to the current manual or diagram.
