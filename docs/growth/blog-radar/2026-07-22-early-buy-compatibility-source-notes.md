## Why this topic won

- It is newer than the 2026-07-20 remote-diagnostics post and leans into a different workflow problem: ordering discipline.
- It has a direct field payoff for pool and spa techs: fewer wrong-part orders, cleaner replacement planning, and better retrofit decisions.
- It gives SplashLens a conservative, credible role as the proof-first layer before an office order, distributor pickup, or repair quote.

## Key takeaway

The strongest original angle is not "buy early because discounts exist." It is "buy early only after you capture the proof that makes a pump, controller, or retrofit order defensible." Recent sources point to the same pressure:

- PoolPro says early-buy planning now drives spring continuity, SKU reviews, and warranty-risk decisions.
- DOE says small-size dedicated-purpose pool pump motor enforcement gets a delay window, but the variable-speed direction and the standard-size timeline remain real.
- Recent product pages and trade coverage show that retrofit and control options are expanding, which increases compatibility upside and wrong-order risk at the same time.

## Source notes

1. PoolPro Magazine
   Link: https://poolpromag.com/the-business-of-buying-early/
   Date: June 29, 2026
   Notes:
   - Early buy is described as a strategic business decision, not just a discount event.
   - Service companies are reviewing purchase history, growth forecasts, obsolete SKUs, new product lines, warranty trends, and recurring service problems before committing.
   - The article also stresses storage limits, dead inventory risk, and the value of supplier conversations about SKU quality problems.
   Use in post:
   - Lead source for the "what changed" section.
   - Supports the idea that tech-facing proof matters before fall ordering locks in.

2. U.S. Department of Energy
   Link: https://www.energy.gov/gc/articles/dedicated-purpose-pool-pump-motors-enforcement-policy
   Date: April 24, 2026
   Link: https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-04/2026_DPPPM_Enforcement_Policy.pdf
   Notes:
   - DOE says it will delay enforcement activity for small-size dedicated-purpose pool pump motors made on or after September 28, 2027, but before March 26, 2029.
   - The policy still points back to the existing variable-speed control design requirement.
   - The PDF distinguishes standard-size compliance on and after September 29, 2025 from small-size compliance on and after September 28, 2027.
   Use in post:
   - Keeps the compliance language conservative.
   - Supports the message that a delay is not the same thing as a return to simple like-for-like motor swaps.

3. AQUA Magazine
   Link: https://www.aquamagazine.com/products/article/15828665/new-improved-july-2026
   Date: July 2026 roundup
   Notes:
   - AQUA highlights CCEI's Antea VS as a way to integrate compatible variable-speed pumps into the Vigipool ecosystem.
   - The note frames this as smart control and expanded connectivity, not just another bare pump listing.
   Use in post:
   - Evidence that compatible control layers are expanding around the pump room.
   - Supports the "retrofit options are multiplying" framing.

4. CCEI
   Link: https://www.ccei-pool.com/en-us/blog/antea-vs-smart-control-variable-speed-pump
   Date: March 18, 2026
   Notes:

   Use in post:
   - Field detail for what must be confirmed before a control retrofit gets quoted or ordered.

5. Hayward

6. Pentair
