Identify a pool pump impeller, seal, or basket

A pump impeller, shaft seal, or basket is identified from the pump model plate plus the part's shape, markings, and installed position. A photo alone is not a part number, and this page does not publish one.

Proof already used in PartSnap

The public PartSnap proof library treats a worn impeller as a model-plate and dimension problem: vane shape, shaft-seal context, pump family, and a second photo of the pump label. Baskets and seals on the same wet end need the same plate because HP, voltage, and date range change the fit.

  1. Photograph the pump model plate, including HP or THP, voltage, and date if visible.
  2. Photograph the part in place, then a clean close-up of molded numbers or vane shape.
  3. Record a diameter or other dimension the counter can compare with the current diagram.
  4. Say what is still missing. Do not describe a possible family as a confirmed fit.

No part number on this page

SplashLens does not assign a manufacturer part number for impellers, seal kits, lids, or baskets. Order only after the model and the current parts diagram agree. Electrical and seal work still belongs to a qualified technician.

Questions

Can a pump-part photo confirm the replacement?

No. PartSnap can suggest a possible pump family. The model plate, visible markings, and dimensions still have to match a current parts diagram before anyone orders.

Which pump part number does SplashLens publish?

None. This page lists the proof to capture for an impeller, shaft seal, or basket. It does not publish a manufacturer part number.
