Pool closing declined-work note

A declined-work note should say what was observed, what work was recommended, what the customer chose, and what remains open. Use a time-stamped, customer-safe description with photos; do not invent a cause or predict spring damage. Put the open item in the next crew's handoff. This is documentation, not a waiver or insurance decision.

Four fields that make the note useful

  1. Record the observed condition without assigning an unverified cause.
  2. State the specific recommended work and the reason it matters.
  3. Record the customer decision, date, time, and communication channel.
  4. Attach relevant photos and flag the open item for spring handoff.

For a heater that could not be drained, state the heater model, the specific access or safety issue, whether the heater was operated, and the next qualified-service action. Raypak's winterization guidance explains why trapped water matters, but it does not prove damage at this particular stop. Keep observations separate from predictions.

Example note format

Observed: [model and visible condition]. Recommended: [specific winterization or repair by qualified technician]. Customer decision: [approved / declined / pending], communicated [date, time, channel]. Work completed today: [verified items only]. Open risk: [what remains unverified]. Evidence: [photo references]. Next action: [owner, due date, spring handoff].

This is a blank format, not a real customer record.

Keep the language neutral

Do not write "fully winterized" if an identified component was left open. Avoid legal conclusions, blame, insurance promises, or a claim that a photograph alone proves a future outcome. Use the company's approved customer-approval process.

Questions

Should a declined-work note say the customer caused future damage?

No. Record observed facts, the offered work, the customer's decision, and remaining uncertainty. Do not predict blame, coverage, or a future outcome.

Is a declined-work note an insurance waiver?

No. It is a service record, not insurance or a guarantee. Contract and legal requirements should be handled through the company's approved process.
